Therapeutic approaches to improve effector CD8+ T cell function by limiting or reversing tumor-mediated exhaustion is an area of active investigation. Terminally exhausted CD8+ T cells from tumors exhibit increased transcriptional repression of effector genes by tri-methylation of histone 3 lysine 27 (H3K27me3). Enhancer of Zeste Homolog 2 (Ezh2) is a crucial enzyme acting as the catalytic core of the Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2), which adds H3K27me3 in T cells. Suppression of Ezh2 significantly increased the proportion of CD8+ T cells expressing both TNF and IFNg when exposed to chronic stimulation in vitro or in the tumor. Ezh2-deficient CD8+ T cells also exhibited decreases in the exhaustion-mediated transcription factor TOX. Multiple genes were upregulated when Ezh2 was deleted, including cytokines related to type 1 (e.g. Gzmc) and type 2 (e.g. Il13) immunity and treatment with IL-4 induced a significantly higher proportion of IL-13 expressing CD8+ T cells. These findings suggest that Ezh2 contributes to epigenetic modifications supporting T cell exhaustion, and Ezh2 suppression or deletion can rescue effector functions of exhausted CD8+ T cells. Thus, Ezh2 may be a therapeutic target to improve anti-tumor immunity.

Although HD is a late onset disorder, it was shown to have an early neurodevelopmental component. Here we sought to unveil and characterize early pre-symptomatic alterations of Knockout (KO) HTT and mutant HTT expression. We identified several proteins which interacted exclusively with the mutant (or WT) HTT, including the DNA methyltransferase 3B (DNMT3B). Analysing genome-wide DNA methylation in iPSC-derived organoids, we found global and wide-spread DNA de-methylation in mutant cells.
